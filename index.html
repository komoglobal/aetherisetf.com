AETF / HOG pools
The stable side of HOG liquidity
AETF provides the stable side of HOG liquidity: when HOG dips, the pools buy HOG with AETF; when it rises, they sell. The public AETF/HOG pools on Tinyman, Pact, and LiquiHog STAMM do this automatically as traders swap. Inventory also changes when liquidity is added or withdrawn. This is not a price-support promise, and it is not investment advice.

When HOG dips

The pools buy HOG

Traders sell HOG into the AETF/HOG pools. The pools pay in AETF and are left holding more HOG.

When HOG rises

The pools sell HOG

Traders buy HOG from the pools, paying with AETF. The pools are left holding less HOG.

The pool-implied HOG mark is AETF's USDC pool mark multiplied by the AETF/HOG reserve ratio across these venues. AETF's own dollar mark can move. Past inventory changes do not guarantee future results.

Why AETF
Built to be auditable
AETF is a native ASA with public liquidity pools. The pool-implied mark is observable, but it is not a fixed redemption price or guaranteed NAV.
🔒

Simple asset layer

AETF is a native Algorand ASA with a fixed 10,000-token supply. The trading pools are separate AMM smart-contract systems with their own risks.

⚖️

Publicly observable

Pool balances and transactions are visible on-chain. The AETF/USDC pool supplies a market route and dollar mark, not contractual redemption.

📊

Multi-market basket

USDC, fUSDC, and HOG are connected through public AETF liquidity markets on Tinyman, Pact, and LiquiHog STAMM. Actual weights can drift.

🤖

Market alignment

Permissionless arbitrage may align AETF prices across pools. It does not enforce target weights or guarantee dip-buying.

💧

Multiple exit routes

Nine public pools provide trading routes, but execution depends on depth, fees, market conditions, and slippage.

🏛️

Transparent early-stage system

Fixed supply, public reserves, and on-chain reporting. This is an early-stage token pool, not a registered fund or institutional custody product.
